HP DeskJet 3755 vs Canon Pixma MG2522: Smallest Budget Printers

4 min read · 2026-07-28

Finding room for a printer in a small apartment, dorm room, or tucked-away office corner is a constant headache. Traditional home office printers are bulky plastic towers that easily consume half of a standard desk. When every square inch of surface real estate matters, an oversized printer quickly becomes an unwanted clutter generator.

That is why ultra-compact budget printers remain so popular for tight quarters. Two of the most widely discussed models in this category are the HP DeskJet 3755 and the Canon Pixma MG2522. Both promise a tiny physical footprint and basic document printing at an accessible price point, but they approach space efficiency, connectivity, and scanning very differently.

Here is how the HP DeskJet 3755 and Canon Pixma MG2522 stack up when space, functionality, and budget are your primary constraints.

Dimensions and Footprint on a Small Desk

When setting up a workstation on a narrow writing desk or a dedicated compact standing desk, physical dimensions are usually the deciding factor.

The HP DeskJet 3755 is widely recognized as one of the smallest all-in-one inkjet printers ever made. With dimensions around 15.8 inches wide, 7.0 inches deep, and 5.5 inches high when completely closed, it takes up remarkably little surface area. Because it utilizes a feed-through scroll scanner rather than a traditional glass flatbed, the top of the machine remains completely flat and shallow. You can easily nestle the HP 3755 onto a narrow wall shelf, a shallow monitor riser, or the back corner of a small desk without encroaching on your keyboard space.

The Canon Pixma MG2522, by contrast, relies on a classic flatbed scanner design. It measures roughly 16.7 inches wide, 12.0 inches deep, and 5.8 inches high. While still significantly smaller than commercial office printers, its 12-inch depth requires a deeper desk surface. Furthermore, because you must lift the top lid to place documents on the glass platen, you need vertical clearance above the unit. That means you cannot easily operate the Canon MG2522 inside a tight bookshelf cubby or directly underneath a low wall mount.

Connectivity: Wireless Flexibility vs. Cable Tethering

How a printer connects to your computer dictates where you can physically put it in a small room.

The HP DeskJet 3755 includes built-in Wi-Fi and mobile printing capabilities via Apple AirPrint and the HP Smart app. Wireless connectivity is a major advantage in compact living quarters. Because you do not need a physical cable attaching the printer to your laptop or desktop, you are not forced to keep the printer on your main workstation. You can store the HP 3755 on a high shelf across the room, on top of a dresser, or tucked inside a storage closet—as long as it reaches a power outlet.

The Canon Pixma MG2522 is a strictly wired, USB-only printer. It does not possess Wi-Fi or Bluetooth capabilities. To print, it must be tethered directly to your computer via a standard USB printer cable (which is frequently sold separately). In a small workspace, a wired connection forces the printer to live right next to your computer, adding another cord to manage across your desk surface and limiting placement options.

Scanning Capabilities: Scroll Feed vs. Flatbed Glass

Both units offer scanning and copying capabilities, but their mechanical designs suit entirely different tasks.

HP solved the size problem on the DeskJet 3755 by implementing a "Scroll Scan" beam. Instead of lifting a lid, you feed individual paper sheets through a narrow slot across the front of the printer.

  • Pros: Eliminates the bulk of a heavy glass lid; keeps the machine ultra-slim.
  • Cons: You can only scan flexible single sheets of standard paper. It cannot scan bound books, thick passports, plastic ID cards, driver's licenses, or delicate photos without risking a jam or poor scan quality.

The Canon Pixma MG2522 features a full-sized glass flatbed platen under its lid.

  • Pros: Highly versatile. You can easily lay down thick textbooks, spiral notebooks, plastic IDs, receipts, and delicate documents flat against the glass.
  • Cons: Demands a larger physical footprint and open overhead clearance.

If your scanning needs are limited to single-page tax forms or contracts, HP's scroll scanner works fine. However, if you are a student or professional who frequently copies textbook pages or ID cards, the traditional flatbed on the Canon is far superior.

Ink Economics and Maintenance

Both models are low-cost entry points, but ongoing ink consumption is worth factoring into your monthly budget.

The HP DeskJet 3755 uses HP 65 ink cartridges and is compatible with the HP Instant Ink subscription service. If you only print a few pages a month, a low-tier ink plan can keep ink costs predictable, with fresh cartridges mailed directly to your door before you run dry.

The Canon Pixma MG2522 uses PG-243 (black) and CL-244 (color) cartridges. The printer itself is often sold at an extremely low price point, making it an attractive quick-buy for emergencies.
