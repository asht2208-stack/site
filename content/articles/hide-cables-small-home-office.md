## Why cables matter more in small spaces

In a large office, a tangle of cords under a desk is out of sight. In a small room, that same tangle is often at eye level from the bed, the couch, or the doorway — it's one of the main reasons a compact office setup can feel messy even when everything else is tidy.

## The three cable zones

Think of cable management in three separate zones rather than one big tangle to solve at once:

**1. Behind the desk (power).** This is where most of the visual clutter lives. A basic cable tray or a run of adhesive clips along the back edge of the desk keeps power strips and chargers off the floor and out of view from the front of the room.

**2. Under the desk (data and charging).** Keyboard, mouse, monitor, and charging cables tend to hang loose here. Running them through a fabric cable sleeve or binding them with velcro straps into one or two bundles turns a dozen visible wires into one or two you barely notice.

**3. Floor crossing (if the desk isn't against a wall).** Any cable crossing open floor is both a trip hazard and the most visually distracting kind of clutter. A slim cable raceway or a rug with a cord channel underneath solves this in a way that clips alone can't.

## A realistic order of operations

1. Unplug everything and physically separate cables by zone before doing anything else — most of the "how do I even start" feeling comes from trying to route cables while they're all still tangled together.
2. Mount your power strip vertically or behind the desk, not on the floor, so zone 1 cables have a fixed anchor point.
3. Bundle zone 2 cables loosely — tight bundling looks neat on day one but makes it painful to unplug or swap a single device later.
4. Only address zone 3 if a cable actually crosses open floor. If your desk is against a wall, you likely don't need this step at all.

## What to skip

Full cable-management furniture (built-in raceways, custom desk grommets) is usually overkill for a small office and adds cost without solving the actual problem, which is almost always zones 1 and 2. Start with clips, sleeves, and a vertical power strip mount before spending on anything more elaborate.

## Bottom line

Cable clutter is one of the cheapest problems to fix in a small home office and one of the highest-impact ones, since it's usually the first thing you or a guest actually sees. Sort by zone, anchor the power strip, bundle loosely, and skip the floor-crossing step unless you genuinely need it.
